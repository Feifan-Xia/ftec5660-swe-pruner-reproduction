All 72 scheduled attempts were executed; each condition exported 23 valid answers out of 24. Mean cumulative API tokens were 125,036 for baseline, 183,425 for pruner, and 140,734 for recovery. Thus, the local pruner condition used 46.7% more tokens than baseline, while recovery used 23.3% fewer than pruner and 12.6% more than baseline. Mean scores among valid exported answers were 9.87, 9.74 and 9.83, respectively. Failure-zero means were 9.46, 9.33 and 9.42. Both paired token-difference bootstrap intervals cross zero, as do the failure-zero score intervals; eight questions provide limited precision. 

The released pruning mechanism worked: 119 pruned observations in the pruner condition retained 53.1% of their original source tokens. Recovery had 68 pruned observations and three autonomous expansion calls across two tasks, with zero repeated lines. These observations establish operational pruning and exact recovery, while the local evaluation does not reproduce the paper's aggregate token-saving direction. Agent trajectories, optional activation and the added recovery interface change subsequent information-seeking behavior, so per-output compression alone does not determine total API usage. 

Three distinct failures remain visible: a fallback routing defect, an answer-export defect, and a reasoning-budget exhaustion. Together with the different LLM, same-model judge, small subset and mid-run infrastructure amendment, these limit causal and population-level claims. The reproducible contribution is the pinned working pipeline, preserved evidence and reusable observation interface; a larger independent evaluation and controlled recovery replays would test whether its potential benefits generalize.
